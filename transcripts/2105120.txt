1. **Layout detects** → "there is a `table` at these coordinates"
2. **OCR runs** on the cropped table image separately → reads cell by cell

**Technically yes, but it is not easy.**

EasyOCR is built on two separate models:
1. **CRAFT** (text detection) — finds where text regions are on the page
2. **CRNN** (recognition) — actually reads the characters inside each region

The recognition model (CRNN) **can** be fine-tuned. The process involves:
1. Collecting a dataset of cropped Bangla text line images with their correct transcriptions
2. Using CLOVA AI's [`deep-text-recognition-benchmark`](https://github.com/clovaai/deep-text-recognition-benchmark) framework to retrain the CRNN
3. Exporting the weights in EasyOCR-compatible format
4. Loading the custom model via `easyocr.Reader(..., model_storage_directory=..., user_network_directory=...)`

**The catch:** EasyOCR does **not** have a built-in fine-tuning API. You have to use the external training framework, which is more work. But the path exists and is documented by the CLOVA AI team.

### What it does (E4 — Semantic Chunking)
Instead of cutting every 500 characters wherever it lands, it:
1. **Splits on sentence boundaries** — both Bangla `।` / `॥` and Latin `. ! ?`
2. **Groups sentences greedily** into chunks up to `cfg['index']['chunk_size']` characters (default 500)

Updated! The splitter now handles **three layers of fallback**:

1. **Primary** — splits on `।` (correct danda), `॥` (double danda), `.`, `!`, `?`
2. **OCR error fallback** — splits on `|` (pipe character — a very common OCR substitution for `।` since they look identical)
3. **Newline fallback** — splits on `\n` and `\n\n` (Tesseract outputs these, EasyOCR sometimes does too)
4. **Hard fallback** — if none of the above find a boundary and the text is longer than 300 characters, it splits on word boundaries every 300 characters. This ensures even completely un-punctuated OCR output still gets chunked into manageable pieces rather than one huge blob.

This makes the chunker resilient to whatever OCR gives us, whether it is perfect or messy.

--- Embedding ---

REJECTED (initially) — a premature, convenience-based conclusion:
This was the clearest case of the AI cutting a comparison short. CHANGED — expanded testing twice:
I asked for two additional rounds of harder queries (cross-lingual concept translation, then Romanized Bangla/Banglish and complex scientific inference) before allowing any conclusion. This mattered: the expanded tests revealed multilingual-e5-large was actually superior — e.g., correctly mapping "Formula for kinetic energy" to the right Bangla chunk where LaBSE failed and matched the wrong formula. Had I accepted the AI's first shortcut, the final embedding choice documented in the form would have been wrong.

ACCEPTED — final choice:
multilingual-e5-large, based on cumulative evidence across three rounds of increasingly difficult real retrieval tests (8/8 correct on the hardest round vs. LaBSE's 7/8).

ACCEPTED — reasoning-only decision, no code needed:
Flat/exact search (FAISS IndexFlatIP) over HNSW approximate search — since our corpus (~990 pages) is small enough to fit in RAM, exact search stays instantaneous with no need to trade accuracy for HNSW's speed benefit.
